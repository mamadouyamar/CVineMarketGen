# GDP in a Monthly Market: the Bridge Equation. Design

**Date:** 2026-09-19. **Status:** approved in chat ("go").

## Goal

Quarterly real GDP growth in the monthly market: a bridge equation on the
quarterly averages of monthly parents, applied to simulated monthly paths;
notebook 14 and the short paper `paper-gdp.tex`.

## The bridge equation

For quarter $q$, with $\bar{\mathbf{z}}_q$ the mean over the three months of
the monthly parents $\mathbf{z}_t$ and $g_q = 400\,\Delta \log \mathrm{GDP}_q$
the annualized growth in percent,

    g_q = c + gamma' zbar_q + sum_i phi_i g_{q-i} + sigma * eta_q,

estimated by least squares on the history (quarters where all three months
are observed). The innovation `eta` is quarterly, so it is not in the monthly
vine: it is drawn independently of the monthly residuals, from a Johnson SU
fitted to the residual's skewness and kurtosis (with the kurtosis floor), as
the factor-model residuals of notebook 08 are. This independence is the one
assumption of the construction and is stated in the paper.

Probe (134 quarters, 1993Q1-2026Q2): parents payroll growth (`d_payems`,
1200 dlog PAYEMS), industrial-production growth (`d_indpro`) and the change
of the unemployment rate (`d_unrate`), one lag: R2 0.85 (0.49 without 2020Q2-Q3),
sigma 1.75, residual passes the i.i.d. tests.

## Package additions

- `cvinemarketgen/bridge.py`: `Bridge(parents, lags=1)` with `fit(y_q, X_m)`
  (y: Series with quarterly PeriodIndex; X: DataFrame with monthly PeriodIndex;
  the quarterly means of X are computed inside, quarters with fewer than three
  months dropped), attributes `const`, `gamma` (Series), `phi` (list), `sigma`,
  `r2`, `tstat`, `n_obs`, `resid` (Series), `residual` (Johnson SU dict),
  `report`; `simulate(P, y_hist, seed=None)` for a monthly `Paths` whose
  columns include the parents and whose first month follows a quarter end:
  months are grouped by three (the remainder dropped), each quarter's parent
  means go through the equation with the lag(s) from `y_hist` then from the
  path, the innovation drawn from the Johnson SU; returns a `Paths`
  `(n_paths, horizon // 3, 1)` with column `'gdp'` in percent annualized;
  `to_dict`/`from_dict`.
- Helpers in `bridge.py`: `growth_to_level(Pq, base=100.0)` (level paths
  `base * exp(cumsum(g / 400))`, array `(n_paths, quarters)`), and
  `recession_probability(Pq, k=2)` (share of paths with `k` consecutive
  negative quarters within the horizon, and by quarter the share of paths in
  which the `k`-quarter run ends there).
- `load_fred_quarterly(series_ids, start, end, cache, refresh, verbose)` in
  `data.py`: quarterly means of FRED series (quarterly series such as GDPC1
  pass through), `PeriodIndex('Q')`, same cache logic as the monthly loader.
- Exports in `__init__`; tests `tests/test_bridge.py` (synthetic monthly
  parents and a quarterly target generated by the equation: recovery of the
  coefficients, shapes, level and recession helpers, JSON round trip) and a
  quarterly-loader offline test.

## Notebook 14 (`examples/14_gdp_bridge.ipynb`, about 6 minutes)

1. Load: monthly PAYEMS, INDPRO, UNRATE (since 1992-12), SPY since 1993-10;
   quarterly GDPC1; build the parents; truncate the monthly sample to the last
   complete quarter.
2. The bridge equation: coefficients and t-statistics, R2, sigma, residual
   tests, the fitted against actual growth (figure), the same equation without
   2020Q2-Q3 side by side.
3. The monthly market: block on the three parents (`'vecm'`, rank by the
   test), SPY `'ar1-garch'`, `exclude=['2020-03', ..., '2020-06']`; report,
   residual moments and correlations, edges.
4. Paths: 1,000 monthly paths of 24 months; `Bridge.simulate` gives eight
   quarters of GDP growth; fans of quarterly growth and of the GDP level;
   quantile table; probability of two consecutive negative quarters.
5. A payroll scenario: payroll growth lowered by 1.5 points (annualized) for
   six months through the innovation of the block; GDP response.
6. With and without the pandemic quarters in the bridge estimation: the two
   fans of growth compared.
7. Save; where to go next.

## Paper (`paper-gdp.tex`)

Introduction (output is quarterly, markets are monthly; the bridge equation is
the nowcasting literature's answer, Baffigi-Golinelli-Parigi 2004, Giannone-
Reichlin-Small 2008 for the factor alternative, Ghysels-Santa-Clara-Valkanov
2004 for MIDAS); the generator in brief; the bridge equation as a child at its
own frequency (the aggregation, the estimation, the innovation outside the
vine and why); paths of output (the algorithm from monthly paths to quarters,
the level, the recession event); example (tables); conclusion (MIDAS weights,
the pandemic quarters, feedback from output to the monthly variables).
Export script `paper_gdp_export.py` -> `figs/gdp/`.

## Out of scope

MIDAS weights, monthly interpolation of GDP, feedback from GDP to the monthly
block, real-time vintages.
